SentinelFlow Architecture & Standards

About SentinelFlow NIDS

An explainable, production-ready Network Intrusion Detection System built with Flask, XGBoost, and SHAP feature attribution.

1. Architecture & Telemetry Pipeline

SentinelFlow ingests network traffic sessions and evaluates them across a 41-feature dimensional space. The core engine applies an ensemble pipeline combining:

Unified Data Pipeline

Serialized LabelEncoders, StandardScaler, and feature schema definitions stored in a single joblib pipeline to prevent training-serving drift.

Supervised XGBoost Classifier

Multi-class gradient boosted trees calibrated across 5 primary attack categories (Normal, DoS, Probe, R2L, U2R) with minority class balancing.

SHAP Explainability

TreeExplainer calculates local feature attributions for every prediction, translated in real-time into plain-English human sentences for security analysts.

2. Dataset & Benchmark Limitations

SentinelFlow is evaluated on the standard NSL-KDD benchmark dataset (comprising 125,973 training records and 22,544 test records). While NSL-KDD resolves major redundancy issues present in original KDD Cup 1999 data, critical operational limitations must be noted:

  • No Raw Packet Payloads: Records represent synthesized 2-second connection summaries rather than full raw PCAP packet byte streams.
  • Older Traffic Profiles: Traffic patterns reflect legacy protocols and do not account for modern encrypted protocols (e.g., TLS 1.3, QUIC, HTTP/3).
  • Novel Attack Penalty in Test Split: The KDDTest+ dataset intentionally includes 17 novel attack variations (particularly in R2L and U2R) that are absent from the training set, causing lower recall on these categories.

3. Legal Notice & Ethical Usage

This Network Intrusion Detection System is designed exclusively for defense, network security auditing, academic research, and monitoring of authorized corporate or laboratory networks. Unauthorized network packet interception, traffic sniffing, or applying disruptive firewall policies on networks without explicit administrative authorization is illegal and punishable under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) and international cybersecurity statutes.
